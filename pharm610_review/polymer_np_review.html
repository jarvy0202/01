P3 · Molecular weight

Molecular weight averages

A polymer sample is a mix of chains of different lengths, so "molecular weight" is always an average. Different averages weight the big chains differently.

Mn = ΣNᵢMᵢ / ΣNᵢ     Mw = ΣNᵢMᵢ² / ΣNᵢMᵢ     PDI (Đ) = Mw / Mn ≥ 1
  • Mn (number-average): every chain counts once. Methods that count molecules (end groups by NMR or titration) give Mn.
  • Mw (weight-average): heavier chains count more. Light scattering gives Mw because scattered intensity ∝ M.
  • Mv (viscosity-average): from intrinsic viscosity; sits between Mn and Mw.
  • PDI: 1.0 = perfectly uniform. The MALDI example in the slides: Mn 2310, Mw 2360, Đ = 1.02 (very narrow).
P3 · Slides 3–20

GPC / SEC

Gel permeation (size exclusion) chromatography separates polymer chains by their size in solution (hydrodynamic volume), not by chemistry. The polymer must not interact with the column packing (no adsorption or partition, unlike normal HPLC).

Detectors

1 · Concentration: RI, UV-Vis

RI detects the refractive-index difference between solvent and solvent + sample (split reference/sample cell). Tells you how much polymer elutes, not its MW.

2 · Light scattering (LS)

Scattered intensity ∝ MW, so it measures MW directly (absolute). Bigger M → more scattered light.

3 · Viscometer

Bridge design (DP / IP pressure transducers) measures intrinsic viscosity [η] (IV). Used for universal calibration, Mark–Houwink and branching.

RI ∝ (dn/dc)·c  ·  UV ∝ (dA/dc)·c  ·  LS ∝ Mw·(dn/dc)²·c  ·  Visc ∝ IV·c

dn/dc = refractive index increment. LS signal depends on (dn/dc)², so dn/dc must be known for absolute MW.

Light scattering angles

DetectorAngleBest forCatch
RALS90°Small molecules (≤ 20–30 nm)Large molecules scatter unevenly with angle (intramolecular interference), so it needs correction / extrapolation to 0°. Not for large or asymmetric molecules.
LALS~7°Large molecules (> 20–30 nm)Already close to 0°, so no extrapolation needed. But low signal-to-noise.
MALSmanyAbsolute MW and size (Rg)Angular dependence gives conformation and branching information.

Three ways to calibrate

CalibrationDetectorsStandardsResult
1 · ConventionalOne concentration detector (RI or UV)A series of standards → log M vs elution volume curveRelative. Depends on standard type. Accurate only if standards and sample have the same chemistry; otherwise comparative only.
2 · UniversalViscometer + concentrationSeries of standards + one narrow standard to align detectorsIndependent of standard type, because separation is by hydrodynamic volume ∝ M × [η]
3 · Triple / tetra detectionLS + viscometer + RI (+ UV)Only a single narrow standardNo calibration curve. Absolute, independent of standard.
Trap. Polyethylene and polystyrene with the same MW have different coil sizes in the same solvent, so they elute at different times. That is why conventional calibration with polystyrene standards gives only a relative MW for a different polymer.

Branching and shape

Multi-detector GPC measures directly: LS intensity, refractive index, UV absorbance, viscosity. It calculates directly: MW (from SLS), Rg, concentration, composition, [η]. It derives indirectly: Rh, Mark–Houwink parameters, branching.

Shape factor ρ = Rg / Rh
ρShapeWhy
≈ 0.77Solid uniform sphereDense; solvent can't get into the core (latex, folded globular protein)
≈ 1.5–1.8Flexible random coilLoose, solvent flows through (typical polymer in good/theta solvent)
≥ 2.0Rigid rod / extended chainMass far from centre (DNA, Kevlar)

Rg = how mass is spread around the centre (static; from SLS/MALS). Rh = how the molecule moves through liquid (dynamic; from DLS/viscometry).

P3 · Slide 11

Mark–Houwink equation

[η] = K·Mva   ⇒   log[η] = log K + a·log Mv
Read a value of a. 0.5 → theta solvent (coil just unperturbed). 0.5–0.8 → random coil, higher = better solvent (more swollen). ~2 → rigid rod. Below 0.5 → compact sphere.
P3 · Slides 21–29

Mass spectrometry & MALDI-ToF

  • Spectrum peaks: molecular ion M+•; base peak = the tallest; fragment peaks = more stable cations; isotope peaks M+1, M+2…
  • Hard ionization (molecule can break apart): EI (electron ionization, high-energy electron beam on gas-phase molecules) and CI (chemical ionization with a reagent gas such as methane).
  • Soft ionization (keeps big molecules intact, so it is used for polymers): ESI and MALDI.
  • ESI, 3 steps: (1) spray of fine charged droplets → (2) solvent evaporates → (3) ions ejected from the highly charged droplets.
  • MALDI: laser is fired at a matrix mixed with the sample. The matrix absorbs most of the laser energy and ionizes, then passes charge gently to the analyte.
Why MALDI-ToF for polymers. Soft ionization keeps chains intact; ToF works for very heavy ions. Used for DNA, lipids, proteins, polymers. Gives Mn, Mw, PDI and end-group analysis.
P3 · Slides 30–41

¹H NMR: Mn and copolymer ratio

Four things you read from a ¹H spectrum: chemical shift (environment), integration (area ∝ number of H), splitting (N+1 rule: N neighbouring H give N+1 lines), coupling constant J (line spacing, Hz).

The whole method is just this: area under a peak ∝ number of protons. Divide each area by how many H that group has, and you get relative moles.

End-group analysis → Mn (PEG diacrylate example)

H₂C=CH–CO–(OCH₂CH₂)ₙ–O–CO–CH=CH₂   FW: 55.06 | (44.05)ₙ | 71.06

  1. Integral per proton (from the end groups, which you know there are 2 of): (10.00 + 9.66 + 10.17) / 6 = 4.97
  2. Repeat units n: (20.79 + 151.87) / 4 H = 43.17 → 43.17 / 4.97 = 8.69 (≈ 9)
  3. Mn = end groups + n × repeat unit = (55.06 + 71.06) + 44.05 × 8.69 ≈ 126.1 + 382.8 = ≈ 509 g/mol

One-line version: 6 / (DP × 4) = Iend / Irepeat → DP = 6 × 172.66 / (4 × 29.83) = 8.69. (The slide writes 71.60 for one end group in the final sum; the structure gives 71.06. Either way Mn ≈ 509.)

Copolymer ratio (PEG-b-PPG-b-PEG example)

PPG has a unique CH₃ signal (~1.0 ppm, 3 H). Both blocks overlap at 3.0–4.0 ppm: EG gives 4 H (–CH₂CH₂–), PG gives 3 H (–CH– + –CH₂–).

  1. PG: methyl integral 30 / 3 H = 10
  2. EG: backbone 3.0–4.0 ppm = 31.50 + 18.76 + 13.10 = 63.36. Remove the PG part: PG has 3 backbone H, the same as its 3 methyl H, so subtract 30. (63.36 − 30) / 4 = 8.34
  3. mol % EG = 8.34 / (8.34 + 10) × 100 = 45.5 % (PG = 54.5 %)
Trap. Always divide by the number of H in that group before comparing. And when two signals overlap, subtract the known contribution first (here the PG backbone = PG methyl integral, because both are 3 H).
P3 · Slides 42–43

Titration

% acidic units (copolymer)
  1. 0.5 g copolymer used 5 mL of 1 M NaOH → 5 mmol = 0.005 mol COOH
  2. Mass of acrylic acid = 0.005 × 72 = 0.36 g
  3. 0.36 / 0.5 × 100 = 72 % w/w PAA
Mn by end-group assay

HOOC~~~~COOH + 2 NaOH

  1. 12 g polymer used 6 mL of 1 M NaOH → 0.006 mol COOH
  2. 2 COOH per chain → 0.006 / 2 = 0.003 mol chains
  3. Mn = 12 / 0.003 = 4000 g/mol
Pattern. mol NaOH = M × V(L) = mol acid groups. For Mn: divide by acid groups per chain, then Mn = mass / mol chains. For composition: mol × FW of the acid monomer = mass of acidic units.
Trap. Forgetting to divide by 2 for a chain with two COOH ends doubles your mol of chains and halves Mn.

Nanoparticles: what matters

Definition: at least 50 % of particles have one or more external dimensions between 1 and 100 nm.

Size

Functionalization capacity, fluid drag and diffusion, optical properties, uptake into cells.

Shape (morphology)

Surface binding, cellular uptake and release.

Surface charge

Colloidal stability, aggregation, interactions with the environment.

Porosity

Large internal surface area.

P4 · DLS

Dynamic light scattering (DLS)

Particles in liquid jiggle by Brownian motion. Small particles move fast; big ones move slowly. A laser shines through; the scattered light flickers as particles move. DLS measures how fast the flicker changes → diffusion coefficient D → size.

Stokes–Einstein:   D = kBT / (6πηRh)   ⇒   Rh = kBT / (6πηD)

D diffusion coeff. (m²/s) · kB Boltzmann · T temperature (K) · η viscosity (Pa·s) · Rh hydrodynamic radius (m)

DLS facts

  • Suspension measurement, fast
  • Size = hydrodynamic radius, ≈ 5 nm to ≈ 10 µm
  • Distribution is intensity-based
  • Strongly biased to large particles
  • No shape information

Why biased to big particles

Scattered intensity ∝ d⁶ (Rayleigh). One 3 µm particle out-shines many 1 µm ones.

Reading correlation-function quality (Assignment III)

What you seeMeans
Smooth single decay, intercept ≈ 0.8–0.95, clean baselineGood measurement
Lower intercept + decay shifted to shorter timesMultiple scattering (too concentrated / turbid; size looks too small)
Very low intercept (near 0), uncorrelated background lightFluorescence
Intercept > 1, noisy bumpy tailNumber fluctuations (too few particles, or a few big aggregates)
P4 · SLS

Static light scattering (SLS)

SLS looks at the average scattered intensity (not its flicker). Intensity ∝ M × c, so it gives Mw, Rg (≈ 5 nm to ≈ 1 µm), A2, and the aggregation number of micelles. Also biased to large particles.

Rayleigh:   Kc / Rθ = (1/Mw + 2A2c) · P(θ)     K = 4π²/(λ₀⁴NA) · (n₀ dn/dc)²
  • Rθ Rayleigh ratio (scattered / incident light) · A2 second virial coefficient · P(θ) form factor (angle dependence; depends on size and shape vs wavelength)
  • Small particles (λ ≫ size): P(θ) = 1 → Debye equation: Kc/R = 1/Mw + 2A2c
Trap. SLS gives Rg (mass distribution) and Mw. DLS gives Rh (motion). Don't swap them.

TEM, SEM and Cryo-TEM

Cryo-TEM sample prep

(I) Apply sample to grid and blot to a thin film → (II) plunge-freeze in liquid ethane (≈ −160 °C) → (III) particles trapped in vitrified (glassy) ice.

Electron tomography

Tilt the sample through many angles, take an image at each, rebuild a 3D model (e.g. iron oxide NPs sitting inside liposome bilayers).

Choosing (Assignment III style). Dried surface shape → SEM. Size + internal structure → TEM. Hydrated, near-native, aggregation state → Cryo-TEM. Does shape survive drying → Cryo-TEM vs SEM, compare.
P4 · AFM

Atomic force microscopy (AFM)

  • Works dry and in liquid
  • Resolution: ≈ 10–20 nm lateral, < 1 nm vertical (so trust the height)
  • Number-based size distribution; 3D shape imaging
  • Sample must be on a hard surface
Classic exam question

Why DLS > TEM > AFM height

  • DLS (e.g. 180 nm): hydrodynamic diameter includes the solvation layer / swollen corona; intensity-weighted (d⁶) so big particles push it up.
  • TEM (e.g. 130 nm): dried, 2D projected; hydrated shell collapsed or invisible (low contrast); number-weighted.
  • AFM height (e.g. 95 nm): soft particles flatten on the substrate (adhesion, tip force).
  • Other microscopy differences: SEM metal coating adds a few nm; beam damage shrinks polymers; limited particle counts; image threshold choice.
P4 · Zeta potential

Zeta potential

A charged particle in water attracts a layer of opposite ions. When it moves, some liquid moves with it. The boundary between "moves with the particle" and "stays behind" is the slipping plane. The potential there is the zeta potential (ζ).

Surface chargeZeta potential
DescribesCharge on the particle surfacePotential at the slipping plane
UnitsC/m² or mC/m²mV
Measured byDirect / indirect surface-charge methodsElectrophoretic mobility
Used forSurface chemistry, ionizationColloidal stability, particle interactions

How it's measured: electrophoretic light scattering

Henry:   UE = 2 ε ζ f(κa) / (3η)

UE electrophoretic mobility · ε dielectric constant · ζ zeta potential · η viscosity · f(κa) Henry's function

Technique name: electrophoretic light scattering (ELS) using laser Doppler velocimetry, in a folded capillary cuvette with an electrode at each end.

Practice problems

1 · Titration %: 0.8 g of an acrylic acid copolymer needs 4 mL of 0.5 M NaOH. What % w/w is PAA?

mol NaOH = 0.5 × 0.004 = 0.002 mol COOH → 0.002 × 72 = 0.144 g → 0.144 / 0.8 × 100 = 18 % w/w PAA.

2 · Titration Mn: 5 g of HOOC~~~COOH polymer needs 10 mL of 0.1 M NaOH. Mn?

0.1 × 0.010 = 0.001 mol COOH → ÷ 2 = 0.0005 mol chains → Mn = 5 / 0.0005 = 10,000 g/mol.

3 · NMR end group: PEG diacrylate, vinyl integrals total 12 (6 H), OCH₂CH₂ integral 80. Mn?

Per proton = 12 / 6 = 2. n = (80 / 4) / 2 = 10. Mn = 126.12 + 44.05 × 10 = ≈ 567 g/mol.

4 · NMR ratio: PEG–PPG copolymer, CH₃ (1.0 ppm) integral 60, 3.0–4.0 ppm integral 140. mol % EG?

PG = 60 / 3 = 20. EG = (140 − 60) / 4 = 20. mol % EG = 20 / 40 = 50 %.

5 · SLS: Debye intercept = 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ mol/g, unimer M = 20,000 g/mol. Nagg?

Mw = 1 / 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ = 400,000 g/mol → Nagg = 400,000 / 20,000 = 20.

6 · DLS: D = 4.4 × 10⁻¹² m²/s in water at 25 °C (η = 0.89 mPa·s). Hydrodynamic diameter?

Rh = (1.381 × 10⁻²³ × 298.15) / (6π × 0.89 × 10⁻³ × 4.4 × 10⁻¹²) = 5.58 × 10⁻⁸ m ≈ 56 nm → dh ≈ 112 nm. (Remember: R → multiply by 2 for diameter.)

7 · MALDI: species 1000 Da (N = 1), 2000 Da (N = 2), 3000 Da (N = 1). Mn, Mw, PDI?

Mn = (1000 + 4000 + 3000) / 4 = 2000. Mw = (1×10⁶ + 2×4×10⁶ + 9×10⁶) / 8000 = 18×10⁶ / 8000 = 2250. PDI = 1.125.

8 · Rg = 45 nm, Rh = 58 nm. Shape?

ρ = 45 / 58 = 0.78 → solid sphere.

9 · Mark–Houwink a = 0.72. What does it say?

Flexible random coil in a good solvent (0.5 = theta; ~2 = rod).

10 · MALDI peaks are spaced by 58 Da. Which polymer?

Repeat unit 58 = C₃H₆O → poly(propylene glycol) (PEG would be 44).

Quick self-test

Concept check

In GPC, which elutes first, big or small chains? Why?

Big. They're excluded from the pores and take the shortest path. Small chains enter pores and elute last.

Why is conventional GPC calibration only "relative"?

It converts elution time to MW using standards (often polystyrene). If the sample has different chemistry, the same MW gives a different coil size, so the result is only comparative.

What makes universal calibration universal?

Separation depends on hydrodynamic volume ∝ M × [η]. Measuring [η] with a viscometer makes it independent of standard type.

RALS vs LALS: which for a 100 nm polymer?

LALS (~7°): close to zero angle, so no angular extrapolation needed for large molecules. RALS (90°) suits ≤ 20–30 nm.

Which methods give only Mn?

NMR end-group analysis and titration (they count chain ends = count molecules).

Why is MALDI called "soft" and why does it matter?

The matrix absorbs most of the laser energy, so the polymer ionizes gently without breaking. Each intact chain gives its own peak.

Two ions, same charge, A1 lighter than A2. Which reaches the ToF detector first?

A1 (lighter ions fly faster).

A DLS sample has a few aggregates. What happens to the reported size?

It jumps up. Intensity ∝ d⁶, so the few large aggregates dominate the signal.

DLS gives size from what physical process?

Brownian motion → diffusion coefficient → Stokes–Einstein → Rh.

What can't DLS tell you?

Particle shape.

Which microscope can show internal core–shell structure?

TEM (electrons pass through; also Cryo-TEM).

Biological nanoparticle that can't be dried. Which EM?

Cryo-TEM: flash-frozen, no drying or staining.

Which technique works both dry and in liquid and gives 3D height?

AFM.

Zeta potential is measured where?

At the slipping plane (boundary between the ions moving with the particle and the bulk liquid).

What happens at the isoelectric point?

ζ = 0 → no electrostatic repulsion → particles tend to aggregate.

Name the zeta measurement technique.

Electrophoretic light scattering (ELS) by laser Doppler velocimetry; mobility → ζ via the Henry equation.
